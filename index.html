How it works

  1. 1

    Say what you’re paying for

    A category such as Flights or Groceries, or a shop such as Amazon, Swiggy or Myntra.

  2. 2

    Type the amount

    Your cards are ranked as you type: what each one earns, and what that’s worth to you in rupees.

  3. 3

    Pay with the card on top

    Tap “I’m paying with …” and the payment counts toward that card’s caps, so the next answer is right too.

Your rewards goal

Ranked for what you use rewards for.

A point is worth more as a flight than as money off your bill. Tell Pointly what you use rewards for and it compares every card in rupees for that goal, using the banks’ own published redemption values. Cashback counts at face value.

  • Free tripsPoints count at their flight and hotel value
  • CashbackPoints count only at what they take off your bill
  • Gift vouchersPoints count at their voucher value
  • Max valueEach card counts at its best way to redeem

Setup asks once. Change it any time in Settings.

Caps, counted

It knows when a cap is used up.

Most cards pay their best rate only up to a monthly cap. Tap “I’m paying with …” and Pointly counts the payment toward the card’s caps, so it stops recommending a rate that’s used up and moves the next card up.

Spent on a card outside Pointly? Set a cap’s total by hand in Limits & caps.

Card suggestions

And when a card you don’t have would earn more.

When another card would earn clearly more on a purchase, at least ₹50 or 25% more, Pointly says so, below your own cards and never mixed into them.

Suggestions are chosen only by what they earn. No bank pays for the spot. Already have the card? Add it in one tap. Not for you? Hide it.

Shops and booking channels

Amazon, Swiggy, Myntra or the airline’s own site: where you pay changes what a card earns, so each gets its own answer.

A better way to book

When the same card earns more booked another way, such as the airline’s site instead of a travel site, Pointly says so.
